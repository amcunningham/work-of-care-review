# The Work of Care: lay summary

Anne Marie Cunningham · 20 November 2026 · [Full review](review.md)

This page summarises the review in plain language. The figures come from the main review, where each is reported with its limits. Status: provisional; one reviewer, web searches plus one research database, and many studies read only in summary.

## What we asked

Does the time health services spend deciding who gets care, and in what order, take away from the time available to give care?

## Sorting at the GP front door

- Phoning patients first increased contacts with the practice by a third to a half over the next month and saved no money ([Campbell et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2814%2961058-8); [full trial report](https://doi.org/10.3310/hta19130)).
- In 147 practices that went telephone-first, doctors spent slightly more time consulting, with no savings for hospitals ([Newbould et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j4197)).
- Online-first systems are likely to add work unless far more problems are settled in one contact than studies show ([Salisbury et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.2196/18203)).
- Patients and staff generally liked full triage less, and older and poorer patients used it less ([Rolfe et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp24X737601); [Park et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/jan.70037)).
- More contacts may mean that unmet need is being found, so extra work is not automatically waste ([Salisbury et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.2196/18203)).

## Finding people at risk

- Picking out high-risk patients and giving them a case manager did not reduce hospital use or cost ([Stokes et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0132340); [Stokes et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-010468)).
- Routine health checks, and assessments of everyone over 75, made little or no difference to outcomes ([Krogsbøll et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009009.pub3); [Fletcher et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2804%2917353-4)).
- Assessment of frail older people worked best on wards that also controlled the treatment ([Stuck et al. 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0140-6736%2893%2992884-v); [Ellis et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006211.pub3)). Later analysis could not confirm the difference between wards and advisory teams.

## Assessed, then not treated

- In England in 2022–23, 39% of children referred to mental health services had the referral closed before treatment ([Children's Commissioner 2024](https://www.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/?p=34764)).
- For NHS Continuing Healthcare, 18% of those assessed were found eligible ([NHS Clinical Commissioners 2018](https://basw.co.uk/sites/default/files/resources/basw_64703-7.pdf)).
- In the [independent review into mental health conditions, ADHD and autism](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/independent-review-into-mental-health-conditions-adhd-and-autism-final-report) (Fonagy, 2026), 40% of clinicians surveyed reported no ongoing support after diagnosis.

## Separate intake and delay

- In addiction services, admitting people in 1 day instead of 14 cut dropout before treatment from 26% to 4% ([Maddux et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.3109/00952999509002714)).
- Where assessment was done centrally and treatment elsewhere, fewer people reached treatment and outcomes were no better ([Rohrer et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1176/ps.47.11.1233); [Guydish et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0740-5472%2801%2900170-2)). One Australian state reversed the policy after two years ([Berends 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugpo.2019.09.009)).
- A single front door for mental health services gets people in faster and attracts more referrals. It does not shorten the wait for treatment when treatment capacity is the limit ([Rocks et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-020-05463-4); [Fazel et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0250691); [Isaacs et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20095747)).

## Who loses out

- Getting through a gateway takes effort and skill, which people have unequally ([Dixon-Woods et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-6-35); [Dakin et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2024.116885)).
- Women and people with a psychiatric condition were less likely to get from assessment to addiction treatment ([Arfken et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.2002.10399934); [Claus and Kindleberger 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.2002.10399933)).
- When one team both selects and treats, rationing still happens, through advocacy for favoured patients, and is harder to see ([Hughes and Griffiths 1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0277-9536%2896%2900207-9)).

## Where sorting helps

A senior doctor at the emergency department door ([Abdulwahid et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/94877)), "see and treat" ([Rogers et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14700568/)), and a pooled queue for hip and knee replacement ([Damani et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31874866/); [Vo et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35034657/)) all cut waits. In each, the person sorting can start treatment or the procedure is well defined.

## The alternative

- Patients who saw their regular GP went 18% longer before their next visit ([Kajaria-Montag et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2021.02015)).
- Removing triage ("advanced access") gave small gains in England, where waits were already short ([Salisbury et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17688754/)).
- Buurtzorg nurses in the Netherlands used fewer home care hours, but total cost per client was average ([Gray, Sarnak and Burgers 2015](https://edagsorden.regionh.dk/committee_1444/agenda_254707/documents/6aab94a5-e4ce-4b25-a7b6-d7b95962819e.pdf)).
- UK pilots show that staff and patients like the model; they do not show that it is more efficient ([Hegedüs et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijnsa.2022.100061); [Drennan et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-021931); [Lalani et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31818293/)).
- The largest direct comparison, in mental health, found no difference in outcomes between seeing the same psychiatrist in hospital and community and seeing different ones ([Giacco et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/S2045796018000732)).

## Staffing

Triage is usually brought in because a service is short of capacity. Neither triage nor its removal fixes that shortage ([Murray and Berwick 2003](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.289.8.1035); [Newbould et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j4197)). This point is the review's own inference from those sources.

## Also examined

- Referral management centres: benefit is unproven, and in one large evaluation outpatient attendance rose ([Blank et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.3310/hsdr03240)).
- "Failure demand" (work created by the system itself): a plausible idea, supported mainly by consultancy cases and not measured in general practice ([Locality and Seddon 2014](https://ccednet-rcdec.ca/resource/saving-money-by-doing-the-right-thing-why-local-by-default-must-replace-diseconomies-of-scale/); [Walley et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1108/IJHCQA-08-2017-0159)).

## What is missing

No study found measures the share of staff time spent on sorting against treating and compares services on it. Patients' own time is rarely counted.
